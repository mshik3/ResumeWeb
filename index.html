Super Tic-Tac-Toe

I found it senior year of high school (that's me at the whiteboard), brought it to college, and my friend Ryan and I played it constantly on a chalkboard. Every square is its own game of tic-tac-toe, and where you play decides where your opponent has to go next. It turns a kids' game into real strategy. The lit board above is where the next move has to go.

So I built it: play online, with a friend, or against a bot that goes up to Legendary.

Mentle

A daily mental math game. Everyone gets the same twenty problems and one clock, and you're graded on first-try accuracy, curved against everyone who handed it in that day. Honors opens once you've done the daily.

The Pokémon card stack

I'm chasing a 1st Edition Team Rocket set in PSA 10, and collecting is mostly manual work: checking sold prices, watching auctions, figuring out exactly which card is in a photo. I keep building tools to take that work off my plate.

  • Auction sniper. Browser extension that looks up sold prices, finds live eBay auctions, and places snipes.
  • Card recognition agent. Computer vision plus an LLM that reads a card photo and works out which card it is.
  • Deal bot. Watches r/pkmntcgtrades for underpriced graded slabs and pings me on Discord.
  • Sellability tracker. Scrapes my collection daily and tells me what's worth selling.

Beer Olympics scoreboards

I ran the Beer Olympics at a couple of bachelor parties, so I gave them an upgrade: a site that tracked every game, assigned points, and kept a live leaderboard.

The best part was that everyone could check the scores on their own phone. Guys were sending the standings to their wives and girlfriends to show off and screenshotting the leaderboard. People got way more into it once they could see where they stood.

NetWorth Idle

My real finances as a Melvor Idle-style RPG. Ten skills that level from 1 to 99 on the actual RuneScape XP curve, a quest log that's really my financial plan, and Dave Ramsey and Caleb Hammer as in-game advisors. Idle games are about small moves compounding while you're not looking, which is also how money works. Turning it into a game got me hunting for moves I'd never have bothered with.
